# What the pack recommends

Four findings above materiality, one owner each, ordered by the gross profit they
are worth. The figures below are the month's, taken from
`outputs/answer_key.json`.

## 1. Commercial / Pricing — Nakuru: reset the discount authority

**Finding.** Nakuru realised prices **9.87% below** the budgeted price on the
volume it actually sold, costing **KES 40,901.06** of gross profit. The branch has
discounted deeper every month since May 2025.

**Why it matters more than the number.** The branch's gross profit is **42.93%**
below budget — the worst of the three — while its units are close to plan. This is
not a demand problem being solved by price; it is price being spent to defend a
number that was already going to be met.

**Do this.** Reissue the price file at budget, cap discretionary discount at 7%,
and require approval above it. Report realised price against list by branch weekly
for the next quarter, not monthly.

**Success measure.** Nakuru realised price within 3% of budget by the next close,
with units no more than 5% below plan.

## 2. Procurement — Home Care: renegotiate or reprice

**Finding.** Home Care unit costs are **6.56% above** the budgeted cost on the
volume bought, costing **KES 25,934.14**. The step landed in January 2026 and has
persisted every month since, so it is a new price level rather than a one-off.

**Do this.** Re-tender the two largest Home Care lines, and where the cost cannot
move, reprice — a 6.5% cost rise absorbed on a 30% margin is a third of the
margin. Give the supplier a volume commitment only in exchange for a price.

**Success measure.** Home Care cost per unit within 1.5% of budget, or a price
increase that restores the budgeted margin.

## 3. Branch Sales — Mombasa Road: rebuild the trading plan

**Finding.** Below budget on units in **all three of the last three months**,
**10.86% down** on a rolling basis, worth **KES 41,382.41** at budget margins over
the window. This is the one place where volume, not price, is the problem — and
the branch has been losing ground since August 2025.

The trailing window matters here. A single month below budget is noise; three in
a row is a branch that has stopped trading to plan, and it is the difference
between asking a manager what happened and asking them for a new plan.

**Do this.** Rebuild the branch plan from its actual customer base rather than the
original assumption, and decide within the month whether the gap is coverage
(fewer reps or routes), range (stock-outs on the lines that move) or a competitor
that has taken the volume. Each has a different cost to fix.

**Success measure.** A branch plan the branch manager signs, with a monthly units
target derived from customer counts rather than from the group growth rate.

## 4. Category Management — Staples: check the blend is deliberate

**Finding.** Staples moved from **25.00% of budgeted units to 31.56%** actual.
Valuing that blend change at budget margins costs **KES 14,164.16**.

**Read this one carefully.** Staples is the lowest-margin category in the range, so
growth in it is expensive per unit of gross profit — but it is not automatically
wrong. If the shift is a deliberate traffic-driving decision, the answer is to
price the range for it; if it is drift, the answer is to defend the higher-margin
lines.

**Do this.** Confirm whether the shift was planned. If it was not, review promo
depth on Home Care and Snacks and check whether Staples is being pushed by
availability rather than by demand.

**Success measure.** Staples unit share within 2 points of budget next month, or a
documented decision to carry the higher share with a matching price change.

## What not to do

**Do not tell the sales team to sell more.** Volume is *favourable* this month at
**+8,755.42**. The business is roughly on plan for units and 19% behind on profit.
An instruction to grow volume would add margin-poor sales to a problem that is
already about margin.

## The review that closes the loop

At the next close, the pack regenerates itself and the same four rows either move
or do not. That is the point of a control pack: it removes the argument about what
happened, so the meeting can be about whether the actions worked.
